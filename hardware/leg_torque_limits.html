From 40–60% to a flat 80% on every leg.

§ 02 — Why 80% on legs but 40% on arms

The arm hangs. The leg holds.

Arms swing free. Worst-case static load is the arm's own mass plus a held payload — a few N·m at the shoulder pitch, an order of magnitude less at the wrist. The 40–50% ceilings on arm joints sit comfortably above that envelope and below anything that would mean the controller has gone wrong.

Legs are different. A standing humanoid distributes its weight through the kinematic chain; squats, single-leg stance, and any momentum recovery push specific joints — knee in particular — close to their rated limits in normal operation, not just in failure. Set the ceiling too low and the drive clamps mid-step. Set it too high and a runaway controller has authority to break the gearbox before the safety monitor reacts.

On arms, the ceiling is a fence. On legs, it is a dimensionable parameter — the value matters, not just the principle.

80% was chosen as the smallest ceiling that comfortably covers expected peak demand under a stand-and-squat profile, with margin for transient spikes. The next sections capture the math behind that choice, and the load-test data that should validate it.

§ 03 — What 800 actually means

Per-mille of rated current, not torque.

CiA 402 object 0x6072 is "Max Torque." On a typical drive it would be ‰ of rated torque. On the MyActuator V4 family it is ‰ of rated current — a deviation documented in the X6 motor identification field note.

The practical consequence: 800 means the drive's q-axis current is clamped at 80% of nameplate rated current, not 80% of rated torque. Torque follows current through K_t and the gearbox, so output joint torque under the new ceiling is approximately:

Eq. 1 — output torque at ceiling $$ \tau_{joint,\,max} \;\approx\; 0.8 \cdot I_{rated} \cdot K_t \cdot N \cdot \eta $$
I_rated
nameplate rated current (A) — configured over CAN, not EtherCAT
K_t
torque constant (N·m/A) at the rotor — measured, not trusted
N
gear ratio (X4 = 36, X8 = 19.612)
η
gearbox efficiency (~0.85 typical, joint-specific)

Three of those four are not exposed over EtherCAT and need to be pinned down per-motor. See motor_id_protocol.html § 12-13 for why and how.

Don't reason about this in N·m without measuring K_t per joint. Datasheet K_t drifts ±5–10% per unit and varies with temperature. The ceiling is set, audited, and changed in raw ‰-of-current units — not in physics units. Any "we have margin to 50 N·m" claim needs a measured K_t and a logged peak current behind it.
§ 04 — Where it lives in config

Two writes per drive, both matter.

Each per-joint slave YAML sets 0x6072 twice. The SDO arms the drive at startup (one-time write); the RxPDO copy re-asserts the limit every cycle (defends against drive-side state loss). Both must agree, or behavior is undefined.

# left_knee_X8.yaml — excerpt
sdo:
  # Max torque limit (obj 0x6072) — value in 0.1% units of rated torque
  #   800 = 80% of rated torque. CHANGE THIS to allow more/less torque (max 1000 = 100%)
  - {index: 0x6072, sub_index: 0, type: uint16, value: 800}

rpdo:
  - {index: 0x1600, channels: [
      # [2 bytes] Max torque (0x6072) — cycle-level torque limit (redundant with SDO, default 80%)
      - {index: 0x6072, sub_index: 0, type: int16, command_interface: ~, default: 800}
    ]}

The comment text says "rated torque" — it is wrong, kept as-is across all 25 files for diff hygiene, but the real unit is ‰ of rated current per § 03. Fix on a separate cleanup pass; does not affect runtime.

Generated variants

The build also emits ethercat_readonly/ and ethercat_gravcomp/ from the canonical ethercat/ via sed in CMakeLists.txt. The torque ceiling is copied verbatim into both — bumping ethercat/ bumps all three. Manual edits to the generated folders are overwritten on rebuild.

§ 06 — What fails first if exceeded

The drive clamps, the gearbox doesn't.

The ceiling is a phase-current clamp inside the drive. A controller that demands more than 800‰ does not get more — the drive silently saturates and the actual torque feedback (0x6077) plateaus at the limit. The control loop sees a torque deficit and integrates it; if the loop is a position-mode JTC, this manifests as following error growing without bound. If it's an effort-mode controller running gravity compensation, the body sags.

What this means by failure mode, in the order the symptoms appear:

  • Following error or sag — first 100 ms The drive clamps. The mechanical response stops tracking the command. JTC publishes a following-error diagnostic if the deviation exceeds controllers.yaml tolerance.
  • Phase current at 800‰ sustained — seconds Stator I²R losses scale with current squared. At 80% rated current sustained, motor temperature rises monotonically. MyActuator drives have internal thermal protection but it is configured over CAN, not EtherCAT, and the threshold is not visible to ros2_control.
  • Drive thermal fault — tens of seconds, joint-dependent The drive trips, status word reports fault, the joint goes limp. arm_ethercat_safety sees the CiA 402 state transition and triggers the system e-stop.
  • Gearbox damage — never reached if the above works The X4's 36:1 and X8's 19.612:1 reducers are the most expensive component to replace. They tolerate brief overload but degrade under repeated torque cycling at the limit. The 80% ceiling sits below mechanical destruction; the chain above it should trip well before this regime is sustained.
Prior incident — left wrist yaw X4, 2026-04-01. A wrist X4 broke physically during an effort-mode PD test. The failure was not the ceiling — the motor was driven into a kinematic limit under closed-loop torque commands, and the inertia stored at impact exceeded the joint's structural rating. Lesson: a high ceiling is only safe if motion limits, e-stop wiring, and safety-monitor watchdogs are all live. Raising leg ceilings without re-validating the safety chain is the same class of mistake.
§ 07 — Changing the ceiling

If 80% is wrong, here is how to move it.

  • Edit 0x6072 in both the sdo block and the rpdo default of every joint that needs the new value. Skipping the rpdo copy leaves the cycle-level limit at the old value — silent, intermittent.
  • Update the comment text to match (currently says "rated torque" — the unit is ‰ rated current; fix while you are there).
  • colcon build --packages-select arm_real_bringup. The ethercat_readonly/ and ethercat_gravcomp/ variants regenerate from the canonical configs.
  • Bring up position_viewer.launch.py first — drives stay disabled, no torque applied, but the YAML parse path is exercised.
  • Then arm_real.launch.py on a supported robot, repeat the load test in § 05, and update this doc with new peak/headroom numbers.
  • Commit message convention: arm_real_bringup: leg torque ceiling NN% → MM%. Reference this field note in the body.
The ceiling is a number you set with intent. If you change it, leave evidence behind for whoever reads it next.
