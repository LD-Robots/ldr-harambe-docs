§ 01 — The mechanism

A pair of X4 drives, coupled through cams.

Each foot carries two MyActuator X4 drives — call them motor A and motor B — mounted above the ankle. Neither one owns a joint axis. Instead each turns an eccentric cam of radius r_exc that drives a pushrod down to the foot; the two pushrods together produce both ankle pitch and ankle roll.

The coupling is the whole point: both motors move for either joint. Symmetric motion (A and B turn the same way) pitches the foot; antisymmetric motion (A and B turn opposite ways) rolls it. So the controller cannot command a motor directly — it commands a joint (pitch, roll) and a transform splits that onto the two motors. That transform is the subject of this note.

Pitch is the sum of the two cam contributions; roll is the difference. Everything downstream falls out of that one fact.

The transform lives in harambe_ethercat_driver — ankle_linkage.cpp holds the pure kinematics, AnkleLinkageDriver wraps it, and HarambePvtDriver (the PVT system-interface plugin, robot PVT guide) calls it inside read() / write(). Read converts the two motor states to joint state; write converts the two joint commands back to motor targets.

§ 02 — Geometry & parameters

Five numbers describe the whole linkage.

The geometry is passed to the driver as ros2_control hardware parameters in ros2_control_real_pvt.xacro. These are the exact values on the robot — do not round them:

ParamValueMeaning
r_exc0.025eccentricity radius — the cam offset (m)
d_pitch0.03pitch lever arm (m)
d_roll0.056roll lever arm (m)
pitch_sign-1URDF sign correction applied to the pitch output
roll_sign1URDF sign correction applied to the roll output

Symbols used below: θ_A, θ_B are the two motor positions; p, r are the URDF-frame ankle pitch and roll. The linkage-frame angles p_lk = pitch_sign·p and r_lk = roll_sign·r strip the sign correction so the cam math is sign-agnostic.

§ 03 — Forward — motor → joint

Sum for pitch, difference for roll.

Given the two measured motor angles, the foot pose in the linkage frame is an arcsine of the cam-projected sum and difference, scaled by the lever arms:

Eq. 1 — forward position $$ p = \texttt{pitch\_sign}\cdot\arcsin\!\left(\frac{r_{exc}\,(\sin\theta_A + \sin\theta_B)}{2\,d_{pitch}}\right) $$ $$ r = \texttt{roll\_sign}\cdot\arcsin\!\left(\frac{r_{exc}\,(\sin\theta_A - \sin\theta_B)}{2\,d_{roll}}\right) $$

The arcsin argument is clamped to [-1, 1] by a safe_asin helper that logs (throttled) when it has to clamp — a clamp at runtime means the measured motor angles imply a foot pose the linkage geometry can't reach, i.e. a calibration or limit error worth investigating.

§ 04 — Inverse — joint → motor

The write path runs the same math backwards.

To command a joint pose, the driver projects the desired pitch / roll onto each cam, then inverts the arcsine. With p_lk = pitch_sign·p and r_lk = roll_sign·r:

Eq. 2 — inverse position $$ d_A = d_{pitch}\sin p_{lk} + d_{roll}\sin r_{lk}, \qquad d_B = d_{pitch}\sin p_{lk} - d_{roll}\sin r_{lk} $$ $$ \theta_A = \arcsin\!\left(\frac{d_A}{r_{exc}}\right), \qquad \theta_B = \arcsin\!\left(\frac{d_B}{r_{exc}}\right) $$

Same safe_asin clamp guards both motor solutions. Note the structural symmetry with Eq. 1: pitch enters both d_A and d_B with the same sign (the sum), roll enters with opposite signs (the difference).

§ 05 — Velocity & effort

Differentiate the position map; transpose it for torque.

Velocity is the position map differentiated — the cosines of the motor and linkage angles appear because \frac{d}{dx}\arcsin and \frac{d}{dx}\sin bring them in:

Eq. 3 — forward velocity $$ \dot p = \texttt{pitch\_sign}\cdot\frac{r_{exc}\,(\cos\theta_A\,\dot\theta_A + \cos\theta_B\,\dot\theta_B)}{2\,d_{pitch}\cos p_{lk}} $$ $$ \dot r = \texttt{roll\_sign}\cdot\frac{r_{exc}\,(\cos\theta_A\,\dot\theta_A - \cos\theta_B\,\dot\theta_B)}{2\,d_{roll}\cos r_{lk}} $$

Effort maps through the transpose of the velocity Jacobian — the standard duality between a velocity map and the force map across the same linkage. Motor torques τ_A, τ_B become joint torques:

Eq. 4 — forward effort (motor → joint) $$ \tau_p = \texttt{pitch\_sign}\cdot\frac{d_{pitch}\cos p_{lk}}{r_{exc}}\left(\frac{\tau_A}{\cos\theta_A} + \frac{\tau_B}{\cos\theta_B}\right) $$ $$ \tau_r = \texttt{roll\_sign}\cdot\frac{d_{roll}\cos r_{lk}}{r_{exc}}\left(\frac{\tau_A}{\cos\theta_A} - \frac{\tau_B}{\cos\theta_B}\right) $$
Singularities at the cam quarter-turn. Every transform divides by a cosine. When |\cos\theta_A|, |\cos\theta_B|, |\cos p_{lk}| or |\cos r_{lk}| drops below 1e-6 the code falls back to the un-projected mid-point form (e.g. \tau_p = (\tau_A + \tau_B)/2) rather than dividing by zero. Keep the ankle well inside its travel; the ±π/4 position limit exists partly to stay clear of these.
§ 06 — Driver & config wiring

One param pairs the joints; the slave configs do the rest.

The driver learns which two joints form an ankle from a single hardware parameter, semicolon-separated pairs of roll,pitch joint names:

<param name="ankle_linkage_pairs">left_ankle_roll_joint_X4,left_ankle_pitch_joint_X4;right_ankle_roll_joint_X4,right_ankle_pitch_joint_X4</param>
<param name="r_exc">0.025</param>
<param name="d_pitch">0.03</param>
<param name="d_roll">0.056</param>
<param name="pitch_sign">-1</param>
<param name="roll_sign">1</param>

Each of the four ankle joints still has its own per-joint EtherCAT slave config in robot_bringup/config/ethercat_pvt/ (left_ankle_pitch_X4.yaml, left_ankle_roll_X4.yaml, and the two right-side files) — the PVT PDO layout, factors and calibration offsets are per-motor as for every other joint. The linkage transform sits above the slave configs: it runs in read()/write() after the raw motor counts are converted to SI, so the per-joint factors/offsets are unchanged by the coupling.

Position, velocity and effort are transformed joint↔motor. The drive-side PD gains kp / kd are not — they pass through per-motor untouched, because the drive runs its PD law in motor space (each X4 regulates its own shaft). So the pitch joint's gains land on motor A and the roll joint's on motor B. Tune ankle gains with that in mind: a "pitch" gain change is really a per-motor change shared across the coupling.

§ 07 — The right-ankle name swap

The names were on the wrong axes.

Commit c590020 ("swap right ankle pitch and roll") fixed a labelling bug in right_foot_joints.xacro: the right_ankle_pitch_joint_X4 and right_ankle_roll_joint_X4 names were attached to the wrong axes — the joint named "pitch" carried the roll axis origin and vice-versa.

  • The EtherCAT bus enumeration was already correct — the fix only renamed joints in the URDF kinematic tree to line up names with axes.
  • Because ankle_linkage_pairs references joints by name, the names must match the axes the linkage math assumes (pitch = sum, roll = difference). A swapped name would have fed the roll command into the pitch term.
  • The left ankle was correct already; only the right side needed the swap.
§ 08 — Safety limits

±45° of travel, two effort ceilings.

The four ankle joints carry per-joint overrides in robot_safety/config/safety_limits.yaml (the robot_safety supervisor and robot_pvt_controller both read them — see the robot PVT guide §08). Exact values on the robot:

Jointeffort_limit (N·m)sustained (N·m)position (rad)bus (V)
ankle_pitch (L/R)20.016.0±0.78539820.0 – 28.0
ankle_roll (L/R)15.012.0±0.78539820.0 – 28.0
  • ±0.785398 rad = ±45° — the URDF ankle travel limit. Also keeps the linkage clear of the cosine singularities in §05.
  • Roll is held tighter than pitch (15 vs 20 N·m): the roll lever arm d_roll is larger, so the same motor torque produces a different joint torque — and roll is the less-loaded axis in stance.
  • 24 V rail. The X4 ankle drives run from the 24 V rail (envelope 20.0 – 28.0 V), unlike the X6/X8 joints on 48 V (40.0 – 54.0 V). See motor identification report.
These envelopes are first-pass and unvalidated. Like every limit in safety_limits.yaml, the ankle numbers are derived from the EtherCAT max_torque SDO and policy gains, not measured to failure. Validate on hardware before trusting — a wrist X4 broke once at its mechanical limit during a torque test.
