§ 01 — From pendulum to body

Same law in the drive. More joints around it.

The pendulum PVT guide established the pattern: boot the MyActuator drive into PVT (CiA-402 mode 5) and let the drive run the impedance law internally at 1 kHz while the host streams the payload. robot_pvt_control/RobotPVTController is that controller scaled to the whole robot.

Eq. 1 — drive-side impedance law (per joint) $$ \tau_j = K_{p,j}\,(q_{d,j} - q_j) + K_{d,j}\,(\dot q_{d,j} - \dot q_j) + \tau_{ff,j} $$

What changes versus the bench rig:

  • Per-joint everything — Kp, Kd, Kd_damp and the feedforward toggles are vectors over the joint roster, not scalars.
  • Body groups — the active joint set is selected by body_groups/<group>.yaml: arms, arms_waist, legs, or full. Bring up a subset before trusting all 25.
  • A real drive plugin — the bench rig used the generic ICube EtherCAT driver; the robot uses a purpose-built HarambePvtDriver (§02).
  • A coupled ankle — the two ankle joints per foot are not independent motors; see the ankle linkage note.
§ 02 — Interfaces & HarambePvtDriver

Five command interfaces per joint, one drive plugin.

harambe_ethercat_driver/HarambePvtDriver is a ros2_control SystemInterface EtherCAT plugin. Every joint claims five command interfaces and exports a wide state vector, all carried on the PVT PDOs:

DirectionInterfaceObjectNotes
commandposition0x607Adrive PD setpoint (encoder counts after factor)
commandvelocity0x60FFdrive PD velocity setpoint
commandeffort0x6071torque feedforward, added inside the drive
commandkp0x2000drive applies value ×0.001; factor 1000 ⇒ host sends N·m/rad
commandkd0x2001same ×1000 scaling as kp

statestatus_word / error_code0x6041 / 0x603FCiA-402 status + fault

The PDOs are RxPDO 0x1601 (22 B, master→drive) and TxPDO 0x1A02 (22 B, drive→master), one per-joint slave config in robot_bringup/config/ethercat_pvt/. Three SDOs configure each drive at startup: 0x6072 max torque = 800‰ rated current, and 0x60C2 interpolation period = 1\times10^{-3} s ⇒ the 1 ms / 1 kHz cycle. The ×1000 kp/kd scaling and PVT PDO are documented drive-side in the EtherCAT control guide §23 / §28.

HarambePvtDriver derives from an AnkleLinkageDriver base that transforms position/velocity/effort between joint and motor space for the eccentric-pushrod ankles, while passing kp/kd through per-motor. Everything in this guide applies to the ankle joints, but read the ankle linkage note for what "a pitch gain" actually means on a coupled pair.

§ 03 — Modes — incl. GRAVCOMP

Four modes. The fourth is new.

The controller carries the bench rig's FREE / PVT / DAMPING trio, plus a new GRAVCOMP mode for whole-body gravity-compensated back-driving. Each has a std_srvs/srv/Trigger service.

ModeServiceWhat the joints do
FREE~/freezero drive torque — every joint coasts
PVT~/hold + streamingtrack the streamed setpoint / trajectory (Eq. 1)
DAMPING~/dampKp=0, Kd=Kd_damp on every active joint — viscous brake, no position regulation
GRAVCOMP~/gravcompfull PVT, but Kp forced to 0 on joints flagged ff_gravity — they float on gravity FF while the rest keep tracking

GRAVCOMP — selective Kp-zeroing

DAMPING zeros Kp on all active joints; GRAVCOMP zeros K_p only where ff_gravity[j] is true and leaves K_d untouched. Those joints become hand-back-drivable but still hold against gravity through the feedforward channel; any joint without ff_gravity keeps tracking normally. The service reports the split, e.g. "GRAVCOMP — Kp=0 on N of M active joints".

A zeroed Kp must pin the rate limiter to measured q. While K_p=0 the joint drifts away from the stale position command; if the rate limiter kept ramping toward the old setpoint, restoring K_p>0 (e.g. ~/hold out of GRAVCOMP) would slam the joint across the accumulated error. So on every cycle a gravity-comped joint seeds its rate limiter to the measured position. This is the same invariant the DAMPING path relies on.

All four services validate lifecycle state first — they refuse with "controller not active; refuse to <MODE>" if the controller isn't ACTIVE, so a mode call during bringup or after an e-stop can't touch unclaimed interfaces.

§ 04 — Endpoint hold & start-knot

Hold the commanded endpoint — except when you shouldn't.

Two related fixes govern how a joint behaves at the seams of a trajectory — at completion and at the implicit start knot. Both turn on one distinction: is the joint actively position-controlled, or back-driven?

Endpoint hold

  • Clean completion → hold the commanded trajectory endpoint (where the rate limiter converged). An under-tuned joint has steady-state following error; holding measured would zero the K_p(q_d-q) term and let it sag. Holding the command preserves the holding torque and keeps the error visible for tuning — standard JTC "hold last setpoint" semantics.
  • Abort / cancel / e-stop / mode change → promote the measured position (promote_measured_to_setpoint()). A stale endpoint would slam a back-driven or braked joint the moment Kp returns.

Start knot

A single-point goal is prepended with an implicit start knot. Its position is chosen the same way: if the controller is actively holding in PVT (mode == PVT && !waiting_for_setpoint) it seeds from the commanded hold position — starting from measured would inject a step equal to the following error and jerk the joint. In FREE / DAMP / GRAVCOMP or post-e-stop the joint is back-driven, the setpoint is stale, so it seeds from measured.

The rule in one line: position-controlled → trust the command; back-driven → trust the measurement.
§ 05 — Real-time deployment

1 kHz, locked memory, pinned core.

Whole-body PVT runs the controller-manager at 1 kHz with hard real-time settings in controllers_pvt.yaml:

controller_manager:
  ros__parameters:
    update_rate: 1000      # PVT at 1 kHz — slave configs set the 1 ms cycle
    lock_memory: true      # mlockall — kills page-fault stalls
    thread_priority: 80    # SCHED_FIFO; above the NIC IRQ thread
    cpu_affinity: 3       # pin to an isolated core (isolcpus)
SettingValueWhy
update_rate1000matches the drives' 1 ms interpolation period; PD runs in the drive, host streams every cycle
lock_memorytruemlockall() — no page-fault stalls in the RT loop
thread_priority80SCHED_FIFO, raised above the EtherCAT NIC IRQ thread
cpu_affinity3pin the control thread to an isolated core (boot isolcpus)
The controller spawns inactive. robot_pvt_controller comes up loaded-but-inactive. The operator activates it only after confirming telemetry is healthy (temps, bus voltage, error codes all green) — there is no auto-activation onto a 25-joint robot.
§ 06 — Tuning diagnostics

What the loop asked for vs what it sent.

Two per-cycle publishers expose the inside of the control loop, each a trajectory_msgs/JointTrajectoryPoint indexed in joint-roster order, wrapped in a non-blocking RealtimePublisher:

TopicCarriesTap point
~/referenceref position / velocity / accelerationafter the lag governor, before the rate limiter
~/commandcmd position / velocityafter the rate limiter and safety clamps — what actually goes on the wire

The gap between ~/reference and ~/command is exactly what the rate limiter and clamps removed — the signal to watch when a joint won't track: a reference the limiter is throttling reads very differently from a command the drive is ignoring.

§ 07 — Launching

One launch for torque, one for looking.

# Full PVT stack — drives enabled. Pick a body group; default is arms.
ros2 launch robot_bringup robot_pvt.launch.py robot_group:=arms
ros2 launch robot_bringup robot_pvt.launch.py robot_group:=full use_rviz:=false  # headless NUC

# Read-only viewer — drives stay disabled, no torque output.
ros2 launch robot_bringup robot_pvt_viewer.launch.py
  • robot_group — arms (default), arms_waist, legs, or full.
  • use_rviz — true (default) / false for a headless control box.
  • robot_pvt.launch.py brings up the controller-manager, the joint-state broadcasters (raw 1 kHz + filtered), the drive-status broadcaster, the safety supervisor, and the PVT controller (inactive).
  • robot_pvt_viewer.launch.py uses read-only slave configs that keep the drives in SWITCH_ON_DISABLED — back-drive the robot by hand and watch joint state with zero risk. Use it for pre-flight and calibration.
§ 08 — Safety integration

Per-joint envelopes, two bus rails, hot-reloaded.

The controller links librobot_safety the same way the supervisor does, reading safety.global.* + safety.joints.<joint>.* from safety_limits.yaml. The table is held in a RealtimeBuffer<SafetyLimits>: loadSafetyLimits() writes it from on_configure, the RT update() reads it lock-free, so ros2 param set safety.… takes effect on the next cycle without a re-spawn.

Per-joint overrides

Only the fields that differ from safety.global are overridden. The defaults that matter:

Scopeeffort_limitpositionbus_voltage
global12.0±π40.0 – 54.0 V (48 V rail)
wrists (X4)6.0±π20.0 – 28.0 V (24 V rail)
hips pitch/roll (X8)60.0±π40.0 – 54.0 V
knees (X8)50.00.0 – 2.5 (one-way hinge)40.0 – 54.0 V
ankles (X4)20.0 pitch / 15.0 roll±0.785398 (±45°)20.0 – 28.0 V (24 V rail)

The X4 drives (wrists, shoulder-yaw, ankles) run from a 24 V rail; the X6/X8 drives run from 48 V. A single global bus-voltage envelope can't span both without losing under/over-voltage discrimination, so the 24 V joints carry per-joint bus_voltage_min/max overrides. See the motor identification report.

The e-stop action map differs from the bench rig in one place: action.sustained_effort: damp — a loaded joint that holds high effort too long is braked viscously (\tau=-K_{d,damp}\,\dot q on the active group) rather than left to coast under gravity. Position / overspeed / stale-feed faults hold; thermal / manual free. The supervisor also boots start_latched: true with a startup_grace_sec: 2.0 window, so bringup transients can't latch a spurious e-stop and the drives stay limp until ~/reset.

§ 09 — Services & params

The knobs, in one place.

ServiceEffect

Live-tunable parameters

Per-joint Kp, Kd, Kd_damp, the ff_gravity / ff_inertia / ff_viscous toggles and every safety.* limit are re-read each update() via a RealtimeBuffer — ros2 param set applies on the next cycle, no re-spawn. On real hardware new Kp/Kd are streamed to the drives' 0x2000/0x2001 objects the following cycle.
