§ 01 — Source of truth

One spreadsheet, twenty-five rows, one row per drive.

Every MyActuator V4 drive stores its commissioning parameters in flash, configured over CAN with the MyActuator setup software. None of these parameters are readable over EtherCAT at runtime, so the EtherCAT master has to assume values that match what the drives carry — and if those assumptions drift, the torque chain silently miscalibrates. The ODS is the authoritative record of what is actually in flash.

What the ODS confirms (and what the ros2_control YAMLs depend on per drive):

  • Rated current (drive-configured I_rated) — used in the torque conversion T = T‰ · (factor/1000) · Irated · Kt. Differs from the nameplate value on every drive on the robot.
  • Reduction ratio — 36:1 for X4, 19.61:1 for X6/X8. Drive-internal; not in the YAML.
  • Single-resolution value — 16384 pulses/rev at the motor encoder; the drive normalizes to ±65535 = ±180° at the output shaft.
  • Inner-loop PID — Q/D current loops, speed loop, position loop. Drive-internal; not in the YAML.
  • Current/voltage limits — calibration current, max current, stall current, over/under-voltage thresholds, shutdown/resume temperatures.
  • Motor direction flag — Change Motor Direction = FALSE on every row. Direction reversal is handled in the YAML by negating the factor, not by the drive.

What the ODS does not give reliably:

  • K_t — the KT_OUT column (X4=1.2, X6=1.5, X8=2.0) is a setup-software current-loop slider, not the physical torque constant. It equals the Max Torque column on every row, which is the tell. Bench measurement on X6 #0 returned Kt = 2.0095 (4.3 % under the 2.1 datasheet spec), so the datasheet is the right ballpark. The other 24 motors are mounted and have not been bench-identified.
  • Position zero offsets — calibrated empirically with demo_joint_offset against the physical robot pose, then written into each per-joint YAML's offset: fields. The ODS doesn't track this.
§ 02 — Per-family parameters

The arithmetic in one table.

All EtherCAT torque factors collapse to three rows because every drive in a family shares the same K_t and the same I_rated. src/tools/ethercat_tools/scripts/recompute_torque_factors.py is the single source for the math; never copy these numbers into other configs by hand.

The X6 row carries both the manufacturer-spec value and the bench-identified value for X6 #0 (motor_id_protocol §12). The 4.3 % spread is well inside the magnet/temperature tolerance MyActuator quotes, so the spec value is what the YAML uses across all six X6 drives. The same will be done for X4 and X8 once in-place identification on the assembled robot is possible — see § 04.

Bus voltage — two rails, not one. The 10 X4 drives run from a 24 V rail; the 6 X6 and 9 X8 drives run from a 48 V rail. The safety envelopes per family are 20.0 – 28.0 V (X4) and 40.0 – 54.0 V (X6/X8), both ±~15 % around their nominal. The robot_safety supervisor and the robot_drive_status_broadcaster both carry these envelopes as per-joint overrides — a single global envelope cannot span both rails without losing under/over-voltage discrimination. Overrides live in safety_limits.yaml (safety.joints.<name>.bus_voltage_min/max) and controllers_pvt.yaml / controllers_pvt_viewer.yaml (joint_overrides.<name>.bus_voltage_min/max). Torque-chain math is unaffected — K_t, I_rated, and the resulting factors are voltage-independent.

Direction sign is whatever makes the joint move in the URDF-defined positive sense; it is encoded as the sign of the position/velocity/torque factors in each per-joint YAML, not as a drive-side flag (all 25 drives have Change Motor Direction = FALSE in the ODS). § 04 — Outliers & follow-ups

Three loose ends, in priority order.

right_shoulder_yaw_X4 I_rated is wrong in flash. The drive at bus position 8 carries I_rated = 9.625 A; every other X4 carries 8.625 A. Confirmed typo on the bench-tech side. The YAML's torque factor is derived for 8.625 A — until the drive is re-flashed via the MyActuator CAN setup tool, this joint under-commands by 10.4 % vs the rest. Do this before the joint is run in CST.

Ankle position offsets are still zero. All four ankle X4 drives (bus 17, 18, 23, 24) have offset: 0 on both the command and state position channels. They were never calibrated. Run ros2 run ethercat_tools demo_joint_offset against each ankle at a known reference pose (URDF zero) and write the resulting offsets into the YAML before commanding any non-zero leg pose. The demo_joint_offset tool is the canonical procedure — see ethercat_control_guide.

X4 and X8 K_t are still on datasheet spec. Only X6 #0 has been bench-identified (motor_id_protocol §10, three-payload static-hold). X6 #0 came in 4.3 % under the 2.1 spec — well inside the ±5–10 % magnet/temperature spread, so spec is a safe provisional choice for the assembly, but the same in-place ID is the right follow-up for X4 and X8 once a procedure exists for measuring K_t with the motor mounted. Until then, expect ±5–10 % torque-magnitude error on X4 and X8 joints; the safety supervisor's effort thresholds in arm_ethercat_safety/config/safety_limits.yaml are the backstop.
