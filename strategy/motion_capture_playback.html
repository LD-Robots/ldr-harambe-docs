§ 01 — What it is, when to reach for it

Hand the robot a motion, get a YAML back.

Three executables and one launch file together form a kinesthetic-teaching pipeline. Put the drives in gravity-comp mode, move the robot through a motion by hand, and the recorder writes a waypoint file. Switch back to position mode and the player drives the same trajectory through the JTC stack. Same YAML works in Gazebo via a third executable.

motion_recorder.py
capture

Subscribes to /joint_states, samples at a chosen rate with a dead-band filter, writes a YAML motion file. Body-group aware.

motion_player.py
replay

Loads a motion YAML, detects which body groups are inside, sends parallel FollowJointTrajectory goals to each group's controller. Direct or MoveIt mode.

motion_player_sim.py
gazebo

Same YAML, different control surface — publishes a 25-element Float64MultiArray to /whole_body_controller/commands with linear interpolation between waypoints.

Use cases

  • Kinesthetic teaching — record gestures, demos, recovery motions by manually moving a gravity-comped arm. The cheapest way to author a motion that a human knows but a planner doesn't.
  • Leg homing — arm_real.launch.py intentionally skips the leg homing nodes; instead, the player's go-to-start ramps legs to the first waypoint of whatever motion plays first. The motion file is the home pose.
  • Demo replay — wave_hello, salut, hug, full_demo. The motions/ directory accumulates these.
  • Regression / sanity — replay a known-good motion in Gazebo to validate URDF or controller-config changes before going to real hardware.
  • Training data seed — recorded motions can seed RL warm-starts or behavior-cloning datasets without needing a planner in the loop.
§ 02 — Two launches, two drive modes

Recording and playback are mutually exclusive.

Recording requires the drives to be in CiA 402 mode 10 (CST — torque control with gravity comp adding the necessary torque to hold against gravity). Playback requires mode 8 (CSP — cyclic synchronous position). The two cannot coexist on the same drive: switching modes mid-session is possible at runtime via the mode_controller, but the launch files configure the drives differently at boot, so in practice you stop one launch, start the other.

PhaseLaunchDrive modeActive controllersSlave configs
recordrecording.launch.pyCST (10)arm/leg effort + JSB + gravity_comp_nodeethercat_gravcomp/
playarm_real.launch.pyCSP (8)arm/leg JTC + JSB + mode_controllerethercat/ (canonical)

Both launches accept robot_group:={arms,arms_waist,legs,full}. Match the recording's group to the playback's group — recording arms-only and playing back as full is fine (legs hold their startup pose), but recording full and playing back as arms drops half the trace.

Gravity comp on the arms is not "free movement" — it's "the operator moves a 0-N·m payload from the arm's perspective." If the comp_scale is wrong the arm sags or floats, and your recorded trajectory carries that bias.
§ 03 — The recorder

Press enter, move the robot, press enter again.

Two-keystroke workflow. The recorder samples /joint_states at the requested rate, drops samples that didn't move (dead-band filter), and writes the result on the second ENTER.

# in one terminal — gravity-comp the arms
ros2 launch arm_real_bringup recording.launch.py

# in another — record
ros2 run dual_arm_moveit_config motion_recorder.py -n wave_hello
# Press ENTER to start … move the robot … press ENTER to stop

Flags worth knowing

FlagDefaultEffect
-n / --namerequiredFilename stem. Output goes to motions/<name>.yaml.
-o / --outputautoOverride output path entirely.
--rate10 HzSampling rate. Bump to 20–50 Hz for fast motions; 10 is fine for demos.
--min-change0.001 radDead-band. Samples within this band of the last waypoint are dropped — keeps the file small during slow phases.
--robot-grouparmsWhich joints to capture. Choices match the launch arg.
--joints—Custom joint list. Overrides --robot-group.
--topic/joint_statesSource topic. Rare to override.
-d / --description—Free-text metadata. Ends up in the YAML header — use it.

What gets written

One waypoint per kept sample, with t in seconds since the first sample and positions as a flow-style array in joint-name order. Positions rounded to 6 decimals, times to 4. The file is loadable as plain YAML with no custom tags.

§ 04 — The motion file

Joint names are the contract.

motion:
  name: wave_hello_4
  recorded_at: '2026-03-04T18:15:54'
  joint_names:
    - left_shoulder_pitch_joint_X6
    - left_shoulder_roll_joint_X6
    - left_shoulder_yaw_joint_X4
    - left_elbow_pitch_joint_X6
    - left_wrist_yaw_joint_X4
    - left_wrist_roll_joint_X4
  sample_rate_hz: 10.0
  total_duration_sec: 66.6145
  num_waypoints: 588
  playback:
    speed_scale: 1.0
    velocity_scaling: 0.3
    acceleration_scaling: 0.3
  waypoints:
    - {t: 0.0,    positions: [-0.055236, -0.162179, 0.342522, -0.157827, -0.060692, -0.047293]}
    - {t: 3.5002, positions: [-0.055236, -0.158632, 0.342546, ...]}
    # …

The player matches motion joint_names against its body-group templates by string. The motor-type suffixes (_X4, _X6, _X8) are part of the name — they are not decoration. A motion captured against the real-hardware joint names will not load against a different URDF that omits the suffixes, and vice versa.

Joint-name mismatch is silent. The player's group detector returns an empty group set if no template matches, and falls through to "no controllers found, exiting." Always run a --dry-run first when you suspect a name mismatch — it prints the detected groups before sending any motion.
§ 05 — The player — real hardware

Detect groups, ramp to start, fire.

The player loads the YAML, scans joint_names against the five body-group templates (left_arm, right_arm, waist, left_leg, right_leg), and sends one FollowJointTrajectory goal per detected group, in parallel, to that group's JTC.

# normal launch — JTC active on every group
ros2 launch arm_real_bringup arm_real.launch.py robot_group:=full

# straight playback
ros2 run dual_arm_moveit_config motion_player.py motions/wave_hello.yaml

# half speed, looping
ros2 run dual_arm_moveit_config motion_player.py motions/wave_hello.yaml --speed 0.5 --loop

# preflight only — no motion sent
ros2 run dual_arm_moveit_config motion_player.py motions/wave_hello.yaml --dry-run

Direct vs MoveIt mode

Aspectdirect (default)moveit
SpeedReplays the original timing scaled by --speed.Slower — each waypoint plans separately.
CollisionNone — raw trajectory.Full collision-aware planning per waypoint.
Waypoint densityAll of them.Auto-decimated to ≤12 waypoints (or --waypoint-step).
Use whenThe motion is known-safe and you want fidelity.The environment changed since recording, or you need IK relaxation.

Go-to-start — what makes the player viable for legs

Before any waypoint streaming, the player checks the gap between current /joint_states and waypoint 0. If the max joint displacement exceeds 0.02 rad it sends a slow ramp trajectory at ≤0.2 rad/s with a 3-second floor, prompting the operator first if displacement exceeds 0.5 rad. This is the leg homing mechanism — arm_real.launch.py deliberately omits the leg homing nodes because the first motion's go-to-start does the work, with the motion's first waypoint as the home pose.

The home pose is not a constant. It is whatever the next motion starts at. Choose first waypoints carefully on leg motions.

Other flags

  • --velocity-scaling / --accel-scaling — MoveIt-mode planning scales. Defaults read from the YAML's playback block (typically 0.3).
  • --controller — override controller name detection. Useful if you renamed a controller.
  • --planning-group — MoveIt group name override.
  • --waypoint-step — MoveIt-mode decimation. 1 = every waypoint (slow), 5 = every fifth.
§ 06 — The player — Gazebo

One controller, twenty-five joints, fixed order.

Gazebo bringup uses a single whole_body_controller (JointGroupPositionController) instead of the per-group JTCs. The sim player publishes Float64MultiArray commands of length 25 in a fixed joint order — left arm 0–5, right arm 6–11, waist 12, left leg 13–18, right leg 19–24 — at a configurable rate (default 50 Hz) with linear interpolation between motion waypoints.

ros2 run full_robot_gazebo motion_player_sim.py motions/wave_hello.yaml
ros2 run full_robot_gazebo motion_player_sim.py motions/wave_hello.yaml --speed 0.5 --loop
ros2 run full_robot_gazebo motion_player_sim.py motions/wave_hello.yaml --rate 100

For joints not in the motion file, the sim player captures their position at startup and holds it for the duration of playback. Recording arms-only and playing in sim leaves the legs frozen wherever Gazebo spawned them — which is generally what you want.

Ramp-up to the first waypoint uses an interpolated timer for --go-to-duration seconds (default 3.0). Same intent as the real-hardware go-to-start, simpler implementation because there is no FollowJointTrajectory action server in the loop.

§ 07 — Workflows

Three patterns that cover most of it.

A. Author a new motion from scratch

  • Stop any running launch on the robot Confirm the bus is idle: ros2 control list_controllers returns nothing or errors.
  • Bring up gravity-comp ros2 launch arm_real_bringup recording.launch.py robot_group:=arms. Wait for "Successful operation" from each controller spawner.
  • Record ros2 run dual_arm_moveit_config motion_recorder.py -n <name> -d "what this is". Press ENTER, do the motion, press ENTER again.
  • Switch to position mode Stop recording.launch.py. Bring up arm_real.launch.py.
  • Dry-run, then play ... motion_player.py motions/<name>.yaml --dry-run. Confirm the detected groups match what you recorded. Then drop --dry-run.

B. Validate a motion in sim before real hardware

  • Bring up Gazebo full robot With whole_body_controller active.
  • Sim-play the YAML ros2 run full_robot_gazebo motion_player_sim.py motions/<name>.yaml. Watch for self-collisions or table contacts that didn't show up during recording.
  • Iterate on the YAML if needed Edit waypoints in-place, or re-record. Sim playback is the cheap loop.

C. Boot the legs into a known stance

  • Bring up arm_real.launch.py with legs included robot_group:=full or legs. Legs sit wherever they powered up — no auto-homing.
  • Play a "stance" motion whose first waypoint is the desired home pose The go-to-start ramp moves the legs there at ≤0.2 rad/s. The rest of the motion is the actual stance trajectory.
§ 08 — What bites

Six things that have, or will.

  • Wrong launch active. Recording during arm_real.launch.py records a frozen pose because the JTC fights every nudge. Playing during recording.launch.py errors out with "no controllers found." Always check ros2 control list_controllers first.
  • Joint-name suffix drift. A motion recorded against _X4/_X6/_X8 suffixes will silently match no body group on a URDF that omits them. --dry-run catches this.
  • Gravity-comp scale wrong. If comp_scale in recording.launch.py is off (mirrored arm signs, drift since last tune), the recorded trajectory carries the bias. The arm sags or floats relative to where the operator moved it.
  • Drive faults during recording. A CST-mode drive that loses sync or trips thermal kicks back to SWITCH_ON_DISABLED. Gravity comp stops, the arm drops. Operator should support the arm before pressing ENTER to start recording, and after pressing ENTER to stop.
  • Loop with environment. --loop with a motion that ends near a payload or table assumes the environment hasn't moved. It has. Use MoveIt mode if you don't trust the recorded path under repeat play.
  • Leg first-waypoint surprise. Because the player ramps legs to the first waypoint, a motion authored with the legs in an awkward pose will move them awkwardly on every run. Audit the first waypoint of every leg motion.
Prior incident — left wrist yaw X4, 2026-04-01. A wrist X4 broke during effort-mode testing under closed-loop torque commands that drove the joint into a kinematic limit. Kinesthetic teaching is not closed-loop torque, but it shares the gravity-comp infrastructure. Validate that the operator, not the controller, is the source of every commanded torque before committing to a long recording session.
